# GPIW 2026 — Global Prize for Innovation in Water

Source: Ennomotive challenge page + `GPIW 2026 Applicant Guidelines.pdf` (both in this repo).

## What it is
International prize run by the Saudi Water Authority (SWA) with TEHA Group. 4th edition,
2026. Open call across the full water value chain, three TRL tracks.

## Tracks
- **Track A — TRL 1–3** (Research & Proof of Concept). Evidence can be experimental
  results, simulations, models, lab findings, or early proof-of-concept — no field
  deployment required. Evaluated on scientific merit, originality, transformative
  potential, future scalability.
- **Track B — TRL 4–7** (Development & Demonstration). Needs lab/pilot/field validation.
- **Track C — TRL 8–9** (Special Mentions, non-monetary; proven/deployed only).

**Track A is our target** — realistic for a solo + AI-assisted effort building a
computational prototype with reproducible simulated results, no physical lab needed.

## Six thematic categories (Track A & B)
1. Advanced Water Production & Desalination Technologies
2. Water Reclamation, Circular Economy & Zero Liquid Discharge (ZLD)
3. Water Efficiency, Conservation & Consumption Management
4. Digitalisation, Automation & Smart Water Management
5. Environmental Sustainability & Water Resource Conservation
6. Accessible & Cost-Effective Wastewater Solutions

One category only per application; can submit multiple applications for genuinely
distinct projects.

## Assessment criteria (8, equal weight)
1. **Technical Excellence** — scientific/engineering foundation, degree of innovation
   vs. state of the art, demonstrated performance/validation/proof of concept, quality
   of methodology.
2. **Scalability & Feasibility** — practicality at scale, cost efficiency, required
   infrastructure/resources/skills/supply chains, regulatory compatibility.
3. **Life-Cycle Impact** — net environmental impact (energy, carbon, water, materials,
   waste, circularity), broader societal benefit.
4. **Durability & Safety** — long-term reliability under real-world conditions, safety,
   risk mitigation.
5. **Clear Market Need** — relevance to an urgent water-sector problem, strength of
   demand, gap/systemic failure addressed, stakeholder validation.
6. **Performance Measurability** — clarity of target outcomes/success metrics, ability
   to quantify performance, credible testing/monitoring/verification, transparency of
   assumptions/data/benchmarks.
7. **Audacious Yet Achievable Vision** — boldness/originality, realistic path from
   concept to deployment, balance of ambition and attainability.
8. **Long-Term Viability & Execution Capacity** — business/adoption model beyond the
   prize, ability to attract partners/investors/customers, team execution credibility,
   IP/licensing/partnership strategy.

## Process & shortlist math
Track A shortlist: top 18 (by Technical Jury score) advance to Board Jury; final score
= weighted average of Technical Jury + Board Jury scores. Highest-ranked Track A
application wins the Grand Prize; next-ranked take the 6 Impact Medals.

## Awards
- Track A: 1 Grand Prize (**USD 100,000**) + 6 Impact Medals (**USD 50,000** each)
- Post-award: Grand Prize + up to 2 Impact Medal winners may get up to
  **USD 250,000** in pilot support — pilot expected to take place in Saudi Arabia,
  using SWA/WTIIRA infrastructure (desalination, reuse, wastewater, distribution).
  This makes **Saudi-relevant deployment framing** a real plus (not required, but a
  differentiator given who funds the pilots).

## Submission requirements
- Pitch deck/technical summary (≤20 slides, PDF/PPT, ≤20MB) — required
- Video (1–2 min, MP4/MOV/AVI, ≤50MB) — required
- Supporting evidence (reports, pilot data, publications) — optional
- English only, no fee, submit via GPIW portal before **Sep 30, 2026**

## Timeline
Applications: Jun 4 – Sep 30, 2026 · Admin screening: Oct 1–10 · Technical eval &
shortlist: Oct 10–31 · Board Jury from Nov 1 · Winners announced Dec 7, 2026 at IDWS
Conference, Jeddah.

## Working thesis (from prior discussion, not yet validated)
"AI for water" alone is too generic — many existing leak-detection/monitoring systems
already exist. Winning Track A likely requires: a genuinely novel technical angle,
demonstrated via a reproducible simulation/computational prototype with quantitative
benchmarks against conventional baselines, realistic deployment economics, and ideally
a story for why it's a good fit for a Saudi pilot (desalination, brine, water scarcity,
energy-intensive treatment, reclaimed wastewater, network losses, etc.).
