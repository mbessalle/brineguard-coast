Regional Solopreneur Positioning & Saudi Water Strategy Fit

Rather than competing on capital-intensive single-vessel hardware, BrineGuard Coast addresses a system-level gap: plant-by-plant outfall assessment can miss cumulative exposure where several desalination plants influence the same receiving water. The Track A prototype will test one representative, multi-plant Saudi coastal corridor with a rolling-horizon production-dispatch optimizer, explicit potable-water constraints, and an uncertainty-aware reduced-order transport model. It is a research proof of concept for future SWA decision support, not yet an operational controller.

Technical Engine: Coordinated Environmental Dispatch Formulation

BrineGuard Coast converts uncoordinated desalination production into a dynamic multi-plant environmental optimization problem. It balances hourly potable-water demand, product-water storage, transmission, and plant-operability constraints against a Reduced-Order Marine Transport Model (ROM). The optimizer determines production rates, active train counts, optional dilution allocations, and product-water storage withdrawals. Brine is not stored: its flow and salt load follow directly from water production and recovery, not from an independently scheduled brine variable.

Optimization Objective

Multi-Objective Cost & Exposure

Minimizing expected operational cost, ramping penalties, and cumulative habitat-area-hours of excess exposure across a forecast-scenario ensemble. Production level is the decision variable — brine flow follows from it (see Marine Kernel card):

min Σω pω { Σi,t [ c_e(t)·E_i(P_i,t) + c_r·|ΔP_i,t| ]
 + w_exp·Σx,t A_x·[S_x,t,ω − S_thr,x]₊·Δt }

$P_i,t$ is plant $i$'s production rate (the control variable) with energy draw $E_i(P_i,t)$; $\omega$ indexes forecast scenarios (currents/tides/wind) with probability $p_\omega$. The exposure term is an auditable salinity-risk proxy — habitat cell area $A_x$ times hours above an illustrative threshold $S_{thr,x}$ — not a claim of biological damage. A CVaR term over $\omega$ is a stretch goal for worst-case robustness.

Transport Physics Surrogate

Reduced-Order Marine Kernel

Fast, scenario-indexed transport kernel ($K_\omega$) mapping brine discharge ($Q_{b,i}$) to seabed excess salinity above ambient ($S_{marine} - S_0$):

S_x,t,ω = S₀,x,t,ω + Σi Στ≤t Kω(x,i,t,τ) · Q_b,i,τ
Q_b,i(t) = P_i(t) · (1 − r_i) / r_i

The time- and scenario-indexed kernel represents nonstationary currents, wind, background salinity, and cumulative far-field plume overlap from a simplified box/advection-diffusion surrogate — not a full 3D CFD run. It is derived and checked offline against open ocean data and reference simulations (e.g. Delft3D/TELEMAC cases); those remain validation references, not a runtime dependency. $Q_{b,i}(t)$ is not an independent decision variable or a stored inventory: it is the brine flow implied by plant $i$'s production $P_i(t)$ and recovery ratio $r_i$. There is no dedicated brine storage — discharge tracks production one-for-one, consistent with real SWRO flexibility (varying production/train count, not accumulating brine).

System Constraints

Storage & Network Balance

Explicit balance governing existing potable-water reservoirs (not new brine storage) and plant/network operational limits:

V_st(t) = V_st(t−1) + Σi P_i(t) − Demand(t) − L(t)
V_min ≤ V_st(t) ≤ V_max
P_i,min·z_i(t) ≤ P_i(t) ≤ P_i,max·z_i(t)
|P_i(t) − P_i(t−1)| ≤ Ramp_i
Σi T_i→j(t) ≤ Transmission_j,max

$z_i(t) \in \{0,1\}$ is plant $i$'s active-train status, gating min/max load; $L(t)$ is network losses. Enforces minimum emergency reserves, plant min/max load, train ramp-rate limits, and transmission capacity between plants. Scope note: the prototype models one representative interconnected multi-plant coastal cluster (illustrative, e.g. a Jubail-style cluster) — this coordination mechanism requires plants to share transmission/a coordinating authority, which does not hold for every Saudi desalination site.
